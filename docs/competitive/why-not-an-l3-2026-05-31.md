# Why zBase Is Not an L3

**Audience:** sales reference for buyer FAQ ("why not Aztec / shh.gg / a privacy L3?") and investor-deck slide source.
**Author:** zBase team
**Date:** 2026-05-31

---

## 1. TL;DR

zBase is an **application on Base and Solana**, not a sovereign chain or rollup. We made that choice on purpose: every privacy L3 we have studied — including the canonical Aztec Connect post-mortem — shows the same failure surface (bridge UX, centralized sequencer, new-chain bootstrap, single-team maintenance burden). The closest live competitor in the L3 lane, [shh.gg](https://shh.gg/docs), inherits most of that surface by construction. zBase wraps the chains users already hold balances on, ships ZK privacy through a Vitalik-co-authored Privacy Pools design, and gates withdrawals through a 3-of-5 threshold-signed ASP — same cryptography, fewer structural single points of failure.

---

## 2. The Aztec Connect post-mortem

**Aztec Connect** — Aztec's first privacy product, a privacy bridge / zkRollup for private DeFi on Ethereum — was **sunset on March 13, 2023** ([Aztec Sunset Memo, 2023](https://medium.com/aztec-protocol/sunsetting-aztec-connect-a786edce5cae)). Deposits were disabled on March 31, 2023 at 14:00 UTC, and the team provided a **one-year fee-free withdrawal window** before stopping rollup-block publication on **March 31, 2024**. After sequencer halt, the memo warned, "withdrawals will be highly technical" — users would have to "run their own sequencers to withdraw" (ibid.).

The official reason was explicitly resource allocation, not regulatory pressure. The memo states: *"We simply are not able to commit sufficient resources to simultaneously support Aztec Connect and the development of an encrypted zkRollup,"* and *"We were under no regulatory pressure nor have we fielded any inquiries from law enforcement"* (ibid.). At sunset, Aztec Connect had served **100,000+ users for private DeFi** (ibid.) — non-trivial reach, but not enough to justify dual-stack maintenance.

It is critical to distinguish two things that are often conflated. **Aztec Connect** (the privacy bridge product) was sunset. **The broader Aztec project** — Noir (the universal ZK language) and the next-generation encrypted zkRollup — was not; it continued and the Aztec mainnet shipped on its own track. The Sunset Memo is explicit that the team chose to redirect engineering toward Noir + a from-scratch private-execution base layer rather than continue patching Aztec Connect.

The lesson is therefore *not* "Aztec failed" — it is that **a privacy bridge running on a centralized sequencer with a small team became unfundable to operate even with 100K users on it**, and a team had to make a clean choice between maintaining it and building the next thing. That trade-off is the pattern any new privacy-infra builder needs to design around.

## 3. The 6-lesson failure pattern

| Aztec Connect reason | What it actually means for any privacy-infra builder |
|---|---|
| 1. Too limited (privacy bridge, not full private smart contracts) | Single-purpose privacy is a wedge, not a destination. A bridge-only product runs out of demand before it pays back its operational cost. |
| 2. Aztec-run centralized sequencer | Centralized operators are a single point of regulatory exposure and uptime risk. One subpoena, one outage, the whole privacy chain is gone. |
| 3. UX too hard (shield/unshield, notes, relayers, proof delays) | Shielding and withdrawing across a separate chain is real product work. If a normal user has to learn "bridge in, then transact, then bridge out," you will lose them. |
| 4. Privacy DeFi had weak network effects | Privacy compounds with depositor count. Thin anonymity sets aren't private. A new chain has to bootstrap that set from zero against incumbents that already own liquidity. |
| 5. Regulatory chill post-Tornado | Even with the Aztec team explicitly denying regulatory pressure, the environment around privacy DeFi changed — wallets, frontends, and CEX off-ramps quietly refuse to integrate, regardless of compliance design. |
| 6. Team can't build + maintain at once | Operating a sovereign privacy chain (sequencer, prover, bridge, relayer, ASP, docs, support) is a full company. A small team cannot do that and ship the next product. Aztec made the right call — sunset and rebuild — but it was forced on them. |

## 4. How shh.gg recreates the Aztec Connect failure surface

**Architecture (verified against [shh.gg/docs](https://shh.gg/docs) and [github.com/privashh/shh](https://github.com/privashh/shh), both fetched 2026-05-31):**

- shh.gg is "a privacy L3 on Base, built on the OP Stack" ([shh.gg/docs](https://shh.gg/docs)).
- It exposes **two profiles**: (A) a full shielded-UTXO chain where "every transfer is a shielded UTXO note. Balances live as commitments; transfers are join-split proofs" (ibid.), and (B) "a transparent EVM L3 with an opt-in, fixed-denomination Privacy Pool. Withdrawals require an Association Set proof" (ibid.).
- It runs "a bidirectional shielded bridge to Base" (ibid.) and operates via "a single sequencer via Docker Compose" / "single-sequencer Docker Compose" for devnet (ibid.).
- ASP gating is present on Profile B; the GitHub org is `privashh` with no individual team members named in the README or contributor list ([github.com/privashh/shh](https://github.com/privashh/shh), fetched 2026-05-31).

**Scoring shh.gg against the 6 lessons:**

| # | Lesson | shh.gg exposure | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single-purpose privacy wedge | **Medium** | Two-profile design (UTXO + Pool) is broader than Aztec Connect, but still a privacy-only chain — same long-term demand-saturation risk. |
| 2 | Centralized sequencer | **High** | Docs describe a single sequencer (Docker Compose). Same structural single op as Aztec Connect — one regulatory or uptime event can stop the chain. |
| 3 | Bridge UX | **High** | "Bidirectional shielded bridge to Base" is exactly the shield/unshield pattern that Aztec's Sunset Memo flagged. Mainstream users won't bridge to get privacy. |
| 4 | Network-effect bootstrap | **High** | A new L3 starts with zero TVL and a zero-depositor anonymity set. It has to bootstrap against Base's existing USDC float — the same uphill climb Aztec Connect ran. |
| 5 | Regulatory chill | **Medium** | Profile B includes ASP gating, which is compliance-friendly. But the team is anonymous (no named individuals in README), which materially raises the bar for CEX/wallet integration — exchanges generally won't integrate with anon-team privacy chains regardless of architecture. |
| 6 | Build + maintain burden | **High** | An OP Stack L3 with bridge, sequencer, ZK circuits, ASP, and docs is the full Aztec Connect surface area, on a small anonymous team. This is the exact failure mode the Sunset Memo named: "not able to commit sufficient resources to simultaneously support [the product] and the development of [the next thing]." |

The shh.gg L3 shape inherits **at minimum** lessons **2, 3, 4, and 6** by construction. None of those are fixable without changing the L3 architecture itself.

This is not a put-down: shh.gg is a real, technically interesting project doing genuine ZK work. The point is structural — the shape of "sovereign privacy L3 + bridge" carries forward Aztec Connect's specific failure surface, and that surface is what we designed around.

## 5. How zBase explicitly avoids each failure mode

zBase is a Privacy Pools fork ([0xbow](https://github.com/0xbow-io/privacy-pools-core) architecture, Apache 2.0, Vitalik co-authored) deployed as an **application** on Base (with a Solana V1 also live). No new chain, no bridge, no sequencer.

| # | Aztec Connect lesson | zBase structural answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Too limited (single product) | Multi-chain by design (Base + Solana V1 live), and the integration target is x402 agent payments + consumer wallets — a category, not a single bridge. Privacy is the wedge; the integration surface is broad. |
| 2 | Centralized sequencer | **No sequencer at all** — zBase is an application on Base/Solana, not a chain. ASP root updates are gated by a **3-of-5 threshold-signed ASP** (shipped in PR #6, code: `ThresholdEntrypoint.sol`), currently in signer recruitment (Soleimani / Kassandra / Wong / Cutler target). |
| 3 | UX too hard (bridge, notes, relayer) | **No bridge.** Users deposit USDC from their existing Base wallet and withdraw to a fresh Base address. Per-payment latency measured at ~7-15s on Base Sepolia after the initial deposit (2026-06-02 re-measurement; the first-time full flow including deposit + ASP update is ~50-75s, which production agents amortize across many subsequent payments). No shield/unshield mental model. |
| 4 | Weak network effects / cold start | **B.3 seed pool** ($1.5K Phase 0, $28K Phase 1) bootstraps the anonymity set from treasury deposits. zBase inherits Base's existing USDC liquidity rather than asking users to bridge into a fresh chain. Anonymity-set disclosure is published. |
| 5 | Regulatory chill | **Named founder** (Eesheng Goh, Base SG DevRel, [lokicheck.com](https://lokicheck.com)). Lineage is the **Vitalik-co-authored Privacy Pools paper** + 0xbow's compliant-by-design ASP. Threshold-signed ASP makes blocklist updates auditable. US Treasury (March 2026) and the GENIUS Act framing recognize legitimate-use mixers; zBase is positioned squarely inside that envelope. |
| 6 | Team can't build + maintain | **Smaller surface area.** An application on existing chains, not a chain itself — no sequencer to run, no bridge to operate, no prover network to bootstrap. PR #6 shipped production-grade gaps (A.1 UTXO notes scaffold, A.2 decoy scheduler, A.3 metadata middleware, B.1 stealth recipients, B.2 threshold ASP, B.3 seed pool, disclosure docs; a yield-distribution variant exists on disk but was never deployed — the live pool has no yield). That cadence is achievable because we don't own a chain. |

---

## 6. The one-line moat

> *"shh.gg recreates the Aztec Connect failure surface (bridge UX + sequencer single-op + new-chain bootstrap). zBase wraps the chains users already use — same crypto, fewer failure modes."*
